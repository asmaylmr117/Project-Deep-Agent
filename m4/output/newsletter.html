Rock

Rock is firmly in the spotlight this week, driven by a blend of legendary longevity and unexpected digital innovation. The Rolling Stones continue to prove their enduring relevance, not only with the release of their latest album, Foreign Tongues, but also by making waves in the virtual space with a dedicated, history-spanning experience on Roblox. Meanwhile, the power of live performance was on full display as Bon Jovi made an emotional and electrifying return to the stage at Madison Square Garden, marking his first public concert since 2022.

The business side of the genre remains just as active; Pophouse Entertainment recently acquired a 50% stake in Iron Maiden’s catalog, signaling a massive vote of confidence in the heavy rock and metal resurgence currently fueled by a new generation of listeners. From 90s-inspired shredding on beabadoobee’s new single "Switchblade" to the collaborative energy of The Linda Lindas and Hayley Williams, rock remains a vibrant, evolving force.

Innovation continues to drive this momentum, with superstars and trendsetters leading the charge. Meanwhile, the genre is also seeing a vibrant celebration of its roots; Argentine sensation Nathy Peluso is taking center stage at the Hollywood Bowl for a highly anticipated "Salsa Spectacular." This performance highlights a broader industry trend of embracing traditional sounds with a contemporary flair, proving that Latin music is not only evolving but masterfully honoring its rich heritage.

Metal

The heavy metal landscape is currently defined by a potent mix of legacy power moves and innovative new voices. Major industry shifts highlight the genre's enduring appeal, most notably with Pophouse Entertainment acquiring a 50% stake in the publishing and master rights of Iron Maiden. This move underscores a broader, statistically significant resurgence in hard rock and metal streaming, largely driven by a new generation of listeners discovering the classics.

On the creative front, the underground continues to thrive with complex, genre-bending releases. Canadian act Solarus has just unveiled their fourth studio effort, Of Sin And Ruin, a masterful blend of power, progressive, and symphonic metal that demands attention. Meanwhile, Chicago’s Black Sites offer a darker, hook-laden take on US metal with their latest, For Eternity, seamlessly integrating gothic and thrash influences. Even the festival circuit is seeing unconventional flair, with William Shatner set to make waves at the 2026 Riot Fest alongside his newly formed metal outfit, The *uckers.

Alternative & Punk

This week we celebrate the enduring spirit of the underground while mourning a true pioneer. The community is reeling from the loss of Dave Kendall, the visionary creator and host of MTV’s 120 Minutes. His dedication to championing left-field artists introduced a generation to the sounds that defined the alternative rock era, leaving an indelible mark on music culture.

On the live circuit, the legendary Mosswood Meltdown returns to Oakland this weekend, promising a defiant, high-energy lineup with headliners Iggy Pop and Bikini Kill. Meanwhile, regional punk scenes continue to thrive; KEXP’s Vinelands program is currently spotlighting the grit and innovation of the Bay Area, featuring local acts like False Flag and Surprise Privilege. As we look ahead, the V&A Museum has announced an immersive "Punk to Pop" exhibition for 2027, ensuring the movement's legacy remains at the forefront of contemporary cultural discourse.
